Neuropathy

Reviewed by Dr. Derrick J. Beech, MD, FACS, Surgical Oncologist
Last updated: September 2026

Neuropathy is nerve damage that causes numbness, tingling, or pain. It is a common side effect of chemotherapy and some targeted therapies.

What Neuropathy Feels Like

  • Tingling or "pins and needles"
  • Numbness
  • Burning or shooting pain
  • Sensitivity to cold
  • Difficulty with fine motor tasks
  • Balance problems

Common Causes

  • Chemotherapy (taxanes, platinum drugs) — see Chemotherapy
  • Targeted therapy
  • Diabetes
  • Vitamin deficiencies

When Symptoms Are Concerning

  • Sudden severe pain
  • Inability to feel feet or hands
  • Difficulty walking
  • Dropping objects frequently

How AskEddy Helps

AskEddy supports patients by explaining neuropathy symptoms, clarifying when follow-up is needed, preparing questions for clinicians, and reducing fear and confusion.
