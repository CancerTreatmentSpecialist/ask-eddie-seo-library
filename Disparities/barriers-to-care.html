Barriers to Cancer Care

Reviewed by Dr. Derrick J. Beech, MD, FACS, Surgical Oncologist
Last updated: September 2026

Barriers to care contribute directly to delayed diagnosis, reduced treatment access, and poorer outcomes across marginalized communities. They affect Indigenous, Black, rural, and low-income communities disproportionately, limiting screening, delaying diagnosis, and reducing access to high-quality treatment.

Major Barriers to Care

  • Transportation barriers — long distances, limited transit, weather constraints
  • Cultural safety issues — care that feels dismissive or disrespectful
  • Screening disparities — lower participation and delayed follow-up
  • Provider bias — assumptions about adherence, cost, or understanding
  • Financial barriers — insurance gaps, treatment delays, out-of-pocket costs
  • Specialist access — oncology deserts in rural and remote regions
  • Health literacy gaps — unclear explanations, rushed appointments
  • Geographic isolation — fewer cancer centers and diagnostic services
  • Mistrust of healthcare systems — rooted in historical and ongoing harm

Impact on Cancer Outcomes

  • Later-stage diagnosis
  • Lower treatment completion
  • Reduced access to advanced therapies
  • Higher emergency presentations
  • Lower survivorship support
  • Increased mortality

Community Strengths

  • Local support networks
  • Community health workers
  • Cultural and land-based healing
  • Faith-based and community organizations
  • Nation-led health governance

Evidence-Based Solutions

  • Mobile screening units
  • Tele-oncology expansion
  • Community-led navigation
  • Trauma-informed care
  • Culturally tailored education
  • Transportation support programs
  • Plain-language communication
  • Partnerships with Indigenous and Black health organizations

How AskEddy Helps

AskEddy reduces barriers by improving clarity, navigation, communication, and confidence — helping patients prepare for appointments, understand results, and advocate for equitable care.
