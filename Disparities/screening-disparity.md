
Cancer Screening Disparities

Screening gaps are one of the strongest drivers of late-stage cancer diagnosis. This page outlines disparities across communities and cancer types.

---

Overview

Screening disparities affect Indigenous, Black, rural, and low‑income populations. These gaps lead to later detection and higher mortality.

---

Key Disparities

• Lower mammography rates
• Lower colonoscopy and FIT test participation
• Lower lung cancer screening eligibility and uptake
• Lower cervical cancer screening
• Delayed follow-up after abnormal results


---

Drivers of Screening Gaps

• Transportation barriers
• Mistrust of healthcare systems
• Lack of culturally safe care
• Insurance and cost barriers
• Limited primary care access
• Provider bias
• Health literacy gaps
• Geographic isolation


---

High‑Impact Cancer Areas

• Breast cancer — lower screening in Black and Indigenous communities
• Colorectal cancer — rural and low-income disparities
• Lung cancer — low uptake even among eligible high-risk patients
• Cervical cancer — lower Pap/HPV screening in marginalized communities


---

Barriers to Screening

• provider bias
• transportation barriers
• financial barriers
• health literacy gaps


---

Evidence‑Based Strategies

• Mobile screening units
• Community-led screening programs
• Culturally tailored outreach
• Navigation support
• Reminder systems
• Telehealth pre-screening visits
• Plain-language education
