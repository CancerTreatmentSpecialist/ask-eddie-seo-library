Clinical Trial Disparities

Clinical trials are essential for advancing cancer treatment, yet many communities are underrepresented — limiting access to new therapies and contributing to unequal outcomes.

---

Overview

Clinical trial disparities occur when certain populations have lower participation, limited access, or reduced eligibility for cancer research studies. These gaps affect treatment options, survival rates, and the development of therapies that work for everyone.

---

Who Is Most Affected

• Indigenous peoples
• Black communities
• Rural residents
• Low-income patients
• Immigrants and newcomers
• Older adults
• Patients with multiple chronic conditions


These groups often face structural barriers that reduce trial access.

---

Key Drivers of Trial Disparities

• Geographic barriers — trials concentrated in large urban cancer centers
• Transportation challenges — long distances to trial sites
• Financial barriers — unpaid time off work, travel costs, childcare
• Provider bias — assumptions about interest or ability to participate
• Strict eligibility criteria — excluding patients with comorbidities
• Health literacy gaps — unclear explanations of risks and benefits
• Historical mistrust — rooted in unethical medical practices
• Limited outreach — few culturally tailored recruitment efforts


---

Impact on Cancer Outcomes

• Reduced access to cutting-edge therapies
• Lower survival rates for certain cancers
• Fewer treatment options for aggressive disease
• Limited representation in research data
• Slower adoption of new treatments in marginalized communities


---

High‑Impact Cancer Areas

Clinical trial disparities are especially significant in:

• Breast cancer — underrepresentation of Black and Indigenous women
• Prostate cancer — limited participation among Black men despite higher risk
• Lung cancer — rural patients often excluded due to travel barriers
• Colorectal cancer — low participation among low-income and rural groups


---

Barriers to Trial Participation

• transportation barriers
• financial barriers
• provider bias
• health literacy gaps
• Limited specialist access
• Complex consent processes


---

Evidence‑Based Solutions

• Decentralized trials (local labs, telehealth visits)
• Community-based recruitment
• Culturally tailored education
• Financial support for travel and lodging
• Simplified consent forms
• Broader eligibility criteria
• Partnerships with Indigenous, Black, and rural health organizations
