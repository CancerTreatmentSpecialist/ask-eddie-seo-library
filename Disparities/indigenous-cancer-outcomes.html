Indigenous Community Cancer Disparities

Reviewed by Dr. Derrick J. Beech, MD, FACS, Surgical Oncologist
Last updated: September 2026

Indigenous peoples across First Nations, Inuit, and Métis communities experience unique cancer disparities shaped by historical trauma, systemic inequities, and barriers to culturally safe care. Indigenous peoples often have lower cancer incidence but higher mortality, largely due to later-stage diagnosis, limited access to specialists, and care environments that do not feel culturally safe.

Key Disparities

  • Later-stage diagnosis
  • Lower screening participation
  • Reduced access to oncology specialists
  • Underrepresentation in clinical trials
  • Higher mortality for colorectal, cervical, and lung cancers
  • Limited survivorship support

Drivers of Inequity

  • Historical and ongoing trauma
  • Geographic isolation
  • Cultural safety gaps
  • Systemic racism
  • Lower access to primary care
  • Socioeconomic barriers
  • Limited integration of traditional healing

Barriers to Care

  • Transportation barriers
  • Cultural safety issues
  • Screening disparities
  • Provider bias
  • Health literacy gaps
  • Specialist access challenges

Community Strengths

  • Strong kinship networks
  • Community-driven wellness programs
  • Land-based healing practices
  • Cultural continuity
  • Traditional medicine knowledge
  • Community health workers
  • Nation-led health governance (e.g., FNHA in BC)

Tsawwassen First Nation Context

  • Healing House provides culturally safe primary care
  • Community programs support fitness, nutrition, cultural connection
  • FNHA collaborates with BC Cancer
  • Screening participation improving but still below provincial averages
  • Navigation support remains a key need

Evidence-Based Strategies

  • Community-led screening
  • Indigenous patient navigators
  • Trauma-informed care
  • Traditional healing integration
  • Tele-oncology
  • Mobile screening units
  • Community-based survivorship planning
  • Plain-language communication

How AskEddy Supports Indigenous Patients
