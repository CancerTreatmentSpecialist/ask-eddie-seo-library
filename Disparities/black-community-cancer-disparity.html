Black Community Cancer Disparities

Reviewed by Dr. Derrick J. Beech, MD, FACS, Surgical Oncologist
Last updated: September 2026

Cancer outcomes in Black communities reflect long-standing inequities in screening, diagnosis, treatment access, and survivorship. Black Americans experience higher mortality for several major cancers despite similar or lower incidence rates. These disparities are driven by structural inequities, delayed diagnosis, under-treatment, and systemic bias.

Key Disparities

  • Higher mortality for breast, prostate, colorectal, and lung cancers
  • Later-stage diagnosis
  • Lower access to high-quality cancer centers
  • Under-treatment (lower surgery, radiation, systemic therapy rates)
  • Lower clinical trial participation
  • Higher burden of comorbidities
  • Reduced survivorship support

Drivers of Inequity

  • Structural racism
  • Provider bias
  • Insurance and financial barriers
  • Geographic access gaps
  • Historical mistrust
  • Lower screening participation
  • Environmental exposures

High-Impact Cancer Areas

  • Breast cancer — 40% higher mortality
  • Prostate cancer — double the mortality
  • Colorectal cancer — later-stage diagnosis
  • Lung cancer — higher mortality despite lower smoking rates

Barriers to Care

  • Screening disparities
  • Provider bias
  • Financial barriers
  • Specialist access
  • Clinical trial exclusion
  • Health literacy gaps

Community Strengths

  • Strong family networks
  • Faith-based support
  • Community health workers
  • Advocacy organizations
  • Culturally tailored screening programs

Evidence-Based Strategies

  • Community-based navigation
  • Bias-reduction training
  • Access to high-quality cancer centers
  • Mobile screening
  • Tele-oncology
  • Plain-language education
  • Improved clinical trial representation
