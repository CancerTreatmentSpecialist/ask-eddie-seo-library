Tumor Markers

Tumor markers are substances — usually proteins — found in blood, urine, or tissue that can help diagnose cancer, monitor treatment, or detect recurrence. They are not perfect tests, but they provide valuable clues that complement biopsies, imaging, and pathology reports.

---

What Tumor Markers Are

Tumor markers are produced by:

• cancer cells
• normal cells reacting to cancer
• organs under stress or inflammation


They are measured through blood tests or tissue analysis.

Tumor markers cannot diagnose cancer alone, but they help guide decisions.

---

Why Tumor Markers Matter

Tumor markers help answer key questions:

• Is cancer present?
• How active is the cancer?
• Is treatment working?
• Has the cancer returned?
• Is targeted therapy an option?


They are especially useful for monitoring cancer over time.

---

Common Tumor Markers and What They Mean

PSA — Prostate Cancer

Prostate‑specific antigen.
Used for screening, diagnosis, and monitoring prostate cancer.
High PSA can also be caused by infection or enlargement.

CA‑125 — Ovarian Cancer

Often elevated in ovarian cancer.
Also rises with menstruation, endometriosis, or inflammation.

CEA — Colorectal Cancer

Carcinoembryonic antigen.
Used to monitor colorectal cancer after treatment.

AFP — Liver and Testicular Cancer

Alpha‑fetoprotein.
Elevated in liver cancer, germ cell tumors, and liver disease.

CA 19‑9 — Pancreatic Cancer

Used to monitor pancreatic cancer progression or recurrence.

HER2 — Breast Cancer

A protein on cancer cells.
Determines whether HER2‑targeted therapy will work.

ER/PR — Breast Cancer

Hormone receptors.
Determine whether hormonal therapy is effective.

LDH — Lymphoma and Melanoma

A marker of cell turnover.
High levels can indicate aggressive disease.

BRAF, EGFR, KRAS — Genetic Mutations

Mutations that guide targeted therapy for lung, colorectal, melanoma, and thyroid cancers.

---

How Tumor Markers Are Used

Diagnosis

Some markers help confirm cancer, but most are not diagnostic alone.

Monitoring Treatment

Falling marker levels often mean treatment is working.

Detecting Recurrence

Rising levels may signal cancer returning before symptoms appear.

Choosing Targeted Therapy

Markers like HER2, EGFR, and BRAF determine which drugs will work.

---

Limitations of Tumor Markers

Tumor markers can be confusing because:

• they can rise for non‑cancer reasons
• normal levels do not always mean cancer is gone
• different labs use different ranges
• they must be interpreted with imaging and pathology
• they vary widely between cancer types


Tumor markers are one piece of the puzzle, not the whole picture.
