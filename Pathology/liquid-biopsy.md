
Liquid Biopsy

A liquid biopsy is a blood test that looks for cancer DNA, RNA, or proteins circulating in the bloodstream. It is a powerful tool for detecting tumor mutations, monitoring treatment, and identifying recurrence — all without needing a traditional tissue biopsy.

---

What a Liquid Biopsy Is

A liquid biopsy analyzes:

• circulating tumor DNA (ctDNA)
• circulating tumor cells (CTCs)
• RNA fragments
• tumor‑related proteins


These fragments enter the bloodstream when cancer cells die or shed material.

Liquid biopsy is minimally invasive, repeatable, and useful when tissue biopsy is difficult.

---

Why Liquid Biopsy Matters

Liquid biopsy helps answer key questions:

• Does the tumor have mutations that can be targeted?
• Is treatment working?
• Has the cancer returned?
• Is the cancer changing over time?
• Are new mutations emerging that affect therapy?


It complements — but does not replace — tissue biopsy.

---

What Liquid Biopsy Can Detect

1. Tumor Mutations (Genomic Profiling)

Examples include:

• EGFR
• KRAS
• BRAF
• PIK3CA
• ALK fusions
• HER2 amplification


These guide targeted therapy decisions.

2. Minimal Residual Disease (MRD)

Detects tiny amounts of cancer after treatment.
Useful in colorectal, breast, lung, and blood cancers.

3. Treatment Resistance

Shows when new mutations appear that make therapy less effective.

4. Recurrence

ctDNA can rise months before imaging shows recurrence.

---

When Liquid Biopsy Is Used

Diagnosis (When Tissue Is Hard to Access)

Examples:

• lung cancer
• liver cancer
• pancreatic cancer
• metastatic disease
• frail patients who cannot undergo biopsy


Treatment Planning

To identify mutations for targeted therapy.

Monitoring Treatment

Falling ctDNA = treatment working
Rising ctDNA = possible progression

Surveillance After Treatment

Detects recurrence early.

---

Limitations of Liquid Biopsy

Liquid biopsy is powerful but not perfect:

• very small tumors may not shed enough DNA
• not all mutations appear in blood
• false negatives can occur
• tissue biopsy is still required for full diagnosis
• results vary between labs


Liquid biopsy is a complement, not a replacement.
