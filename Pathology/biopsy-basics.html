Pathology

Biopsy Basics

Reviewed by Dr. Derrick J. Beech, MD, FACS, Surgical Oncologist
Last updated: September 2026

A biopsy is one of the most important steps in diagnosing cancer. It provides a sample of tissue that pathologists examine under a microscope to determine whether cells are normal, precancerous, or cancerous — and what type of cancer is present.

What a Biopsy Is

A biopsy is a procedure that removes a small piece of tissue or fluid from the body so it can be analyzed. Pathologists study cell appearance, cell organization, growth patterns, markers or proteins, and genetic changes. This information guides diagnosis, treatment planning, and prognosis.

Why Biopsies Matter

  • Is it cancer?
  • What type of cancer is it?
  • How aggressive is it?
  • What treatments might work best?
  • Are there specific mutations or markers?

Biopsies are the foundation of accurate cancer care.

Common Types of Biopsies

  • Needle biopsy — uses a thin needle to remove tissue; often used for breast, lung, liver, or lymph nodes
  • Core biopsy — removes a larger sample with a thicker needle
  • Surgical biopsy — removes part or all of a suspicious area
  • Endoscopic biopsy — taken during colonoscopy, bronchoscopy, or upper endoscopy
  • Skin punch biopsy — used for skin lesions
  • Bone marrow biopsy — used for blood cancers

Each method is chosen based on location, size, and safety.

What Pathologists Look For

Cell Appearance

Are the cells normal, abnormal, precancerous, or cancerous?

Growth Pattern

Are cells organized or growing in a chaotic pattern?

Grade

How aggressive the cancer cells look under the microscope. See Margins & Grade for a full explanation.

Margins

If tissue was removed surgically, are the edges free of cancer?

Receptors and Markers

Examples include ER/PR/HER2 (breast cancer), PD-L1 (immunotherapy response), and Ki-67 (growth rate). See Tumor Markers.

Genetic Mutations

Mutations can guide targeted therapy. See Genetics & Genomics.

What Patients Often Find Confusing

Biopsy reports contain complex terms such as "invasive carcinoma," "high-grade dysplasia," "positive margins," "lymphovascular invasion," "mitotic rate," and "atypia." These terms can be intimidating — but each has a clear meaning that AskEddy can break down.
