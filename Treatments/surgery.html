Cancer Surgery

Reviewed by Dr. Derrick J. Beech, MD, FACS, Surgical Oncologist
Last updated: September 2026

Cancer surgery removes tumors or affected tissue to diagnose, treat, or prevent cancer. It is one of the most common and effective cancer treatments.

Why Surgery Matters

  • Removes the primary tumor
  • Confirms diagnosis through pathology
  • Reduces cancer spread
  • Improves symptoms
  • May cure early-stage cancers

Types of Cancer Surgery

Curative Surgery

Removes all visible cancer.

Debulking Surgery

Removes most of the tumor when full removal isn't possible.

Palliative Surgery

Relieves symptoms (pain, obstruction).

Reconstructive Surgery

Restores appearance or function after treatment.

Diagnostic Surgery

Biopsies to confirm cancer — see Biopsy Basics.

Common Uses

  • Breast cancer (lumpectomy, mastectomy)
  • Colorectal cancer (colon resection)
  • Lung cancer (lobectomy)
  • Skin cancer (excision, Mohs surgery)

What Patients Often Ask

  • "Will I need more treatment after surgery?"
  • "What do margins mean?" — see Margins & Grade
  • "How long is recovery?"
  • "Will lymph nodes be removed?"
