Weight, Obesity & Cancer Risk

Reviewed by Dr. Derrick J. Beech, MD, FACS, Surgical Oncologist
Last updated: September 2026

Being overweight or having obesity is linked to increased risk for at least 13 types of cancer. It is one of the largest modifiable cancer risk factors after tobacco use.

Cancers Linked to Excess Weight

  • Breast cancer (postmenopausal)
  • Colorectal cancer
  • Endometrial cancer
  • Esophageal cancer
  • Kidney cancer
  • Liver cancer
  • Pancreatic cancer
  • Gallbladder cancer
  • Ovarian cancer
  • Thyroid cancer
  • Multiple myeloma
  • Meningioma

How Excess Weight Increases Risk

Fat tissue produces excess estrogen and drives chronic low-grade inflammation, both of which can promote cancer cell growth. Excess weight is also linked to higher insulin levels, which can fuel the growth of certain tumors.

When Weight Matters Most

Evidence suggests that weight gained during adulthood — rather than weight at any single point in time — carries the strongest association with increased risk for several of these cancers.

Weight Loss and Risk

Studies, including those following patients after weight-loss surgery, show that meaningful, sustained weight loss is associated with reduced risk for several obesity-related cancers.

An Important Note

Weight is influenced by many factors beyond individual choice, including genetics, metabolism, and access to healthy food and safe places to be active — several of which overlap with the disparities covered elsewhere in this library. Weight-related risk is one input among many, not a verdict on behavior.
