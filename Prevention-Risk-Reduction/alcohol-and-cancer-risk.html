Alcohol & Cancer Risk

Reviewed by Dr. Derrick J. Beech, MD, FACS, Surgical Oncologist
Last updated: September 2026

Alcohol is a well-established cause of cancer. Risk rises with the amount consumed, and there is no amount of alcohol proven to be completely risk-free when it comes to cancer.

Cancers Linked to Alcohol

  • Mouth and throat cancer
  • Esophageal cancer
  • Liver cancer
  • Breast cancer
  • Colorectal cancer

How Alcohol Increases Risk

The body breaks alcohol down into acetaldehyde, a toxic chemical that can damage DNA. Alcohol can also raise estrogen levels (relevant to breast cancer risk) and impair the body's ability to absorb certain protective nutrients.

Does the Type of Alcohol Matter?

No — beer, wine, and liquor carry similar risk when compared by the amount of pure alcohol consumed. It's the alcohol itself, not the beverage type, that drives the association.

Combined Effects

Alcohol and tobacco use together raise cancer risk more than either factor alone, particularly for cancers of the mouth, throat, and esophagus.

Reducing Risk

Reducing how much and how often you drink lowers risk. For those who drink, limiting intake is the primary evidence-based recommendation; there is no "safe" threshold that eliminates risk entirely.
