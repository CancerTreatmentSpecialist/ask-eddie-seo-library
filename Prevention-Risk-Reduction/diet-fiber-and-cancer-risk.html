Diet, Fiber & Cancer Risk

Reviewed by Dr. Derrick J. Beech, MD, FACS, Surgical Oncologist
Last updated: September 2026

What you eat over the course of years — not any single meal — shapes cancer risk. Diet is most closely tied to colorectal cancer, but also plays a role in several other cancer types.

Fiber and Colorectal Cancer

Diets high in fiber — from whole grains, legumes, fruits, and vegetables — are associated with lower colorectal cancer risk. Fiber speeds digestion, which may reduce the time harmful substances spend in contact with the colon lining.

Red and Processed Meat

Processed meat (bacon, sausage, deli meat) is classified as a carcinogen, and red meat as a probable carcinogen, both linked primarily to colorectal cancer. Cooking methods that char or heavily smoke meat may add further risk.

Fruits and Vegetables

Diets rich in fruits and vegetables are associated with lower risk of several cancers, likely through a combination of fiber, antioxidants, and other protective compounds working together — no single nutrient explains the effect on its own.

Ultra-Processed Foods

Emerging research links diets high in ultra-processed foods to increased cancer risk, though this area of research is still developing compared to the well-established links for tobacco and alcohol.

Practical Takeaways

  • Favor whole grains over refined grains
  • Build meals around vegetables, fruits, and legumes
  • Limit processed and red meat
  • Cook meat using lower-temperature methods when possible
