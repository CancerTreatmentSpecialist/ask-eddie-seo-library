Tobacco & Cancer Risk

Reviewed by Dr. Derrick J. Beech, MD, FACS, Surgical Oncologist
Last updated: September 2026

Tobacco use is the single largest preventable cause of cancer worldwide. It's linked to far more than lung cancer — smoking and other tobacco products damage DNA and increase risk across many parts of the body.

Cancers Linked to Tobacco Use

  • Lung cancer
  • Mouth, throat, and esophageal cancer
  • Bladder cancer
  • Pancreatic cancer
  • Kidney cancer
  • Cervical cancer
  • Acute myeloid leukemia

How Tobacco Increases Risk

Tobacco smoke contains thousands of chemicals, dozens of which are known carcinogens. These substances damage DNA directly and impair the body's ability to repair that damage, allowing abnormal cells to grow unchecked.

It's Not Just Cigarettes

  • Cigars and pipe tobacco carry similar risks
  • Smokeless tobacco (chewing tobacco, snuff) raises oral and pancreatic cancer risk
  • Secondhand smoke increases lung cancer risk in non-smokers
  • The long-term cancer risk of e-cigarettes/vaping is still being studied, but they are not considered risk-free

What Happens When You Quit

Risk begins dropping soon after quitting, and continues declining the longer someone stays smoke-free. Within about 10 years, lung cancer risk is roughly half that of someone who continues smoking. Quitting at any age and after any duration of use provides a benefit.
