Physical Activity & Cancer Risk

Reviewed by Dr. Derrick J. Beech, MD, FACS, Surgical Oncologist
Last updated: September 2026

Regular physical activity is one of the most consistently supported ways to lower cancer risk, independent of its effect on weight.

Cancers Most Affected

  • Colorectal cancer
  • Breast cancer
  • Endometrial cancer
  • Bladder cancer
  • Esophageal cancer
  • Kidney cancer
  • Stomach cancer

How Activity Lowers Risk

Exercise helps regulate hormones (including estrogen and insulin), reduces chronic inflammation, supports immune function, and helps maintain a healthy weight — all pathways connected to cancer development.

How Much Is Enough?

General guidelines recommend at least 150–300 minutes of moderate activity (or 75–150 minutes of vigorous activity) per week. Importantly, some activity is better than none — benefits begin well below these targets, and more activity generally continues to lower risk further.

What Counts as Activity

  • Brisk walking
  • Cycling
  • Swimming
  • Strength training
  • Household and yard work that raises your heart rate

Reducing Sedentary Time

Independent of formal exercise, prolonged sitting is linked to higher cancer risk. Breaking up long periods of sitting with brief movement appears to help, even for people who also exercise regularly.
