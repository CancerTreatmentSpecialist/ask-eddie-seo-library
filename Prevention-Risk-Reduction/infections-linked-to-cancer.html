Infections Linked to Cancer

Reviewed by Dr. Derrick J. Beech, MD, FACS, Surgical Oncologist
Last updated: September 2026

Several common infections are linked to increased cancer risk. Unlike most risk factors, many of these are preventable through vaccination or treatable through medication — making this one of the most actionable categories of cancer prevention.

HPV (Human Papillomavirus)

HPV is the primary cause of cervical cancer and is also linked to cancers of the vulva, vagina, penis, anus, and throat. The HPV vaccine, given ideally before exposure, prevents infection with the HPV strains responsible for most HPV-related cancers. See Cervical Cancer Screening for ongoing monitoring even after vaccination.

Hepatitis B and Hepatitis C

Chronic infection with hepatitis B or hepatitis C is a leading cause of liver cancer worldwide. Hepatitis B is preventable through vaccination. Hepatitis C has no vaccine, but antiviral treatments can cure the infection and substantially reduce liver cancer risk. See Liver Cancer Screening for surveillance guidance if you have chronic hepatitis or cirrhosis.

H. pylori (Helicobacter pylori)

This common stomach bacterium is linked to increased risk of stomach cancer. H. pylori infection is treatable with a course of antibiotics, and treating it can lower future stomach cancer risk in infected individuals.

Other Infections of Note

  • Epstein-Barr virus (EBV) — linked to certain lymphomas and nasopharyngeal cancer
  • HIV — associated with several cancers, largely due to its effects on the immune system

Why This Category Matters

Infection-related cancer risk is unusual among risk factors in that it is often directly preventable (vaccination) or reversible (antiviral or antibiotic treatment) — making early testing and treatment genuinely protective.

How AskEddy Helps

AskEddy supports patients by explaining infection-related cancer risk, clarifying vaccination and testing options, and helping patients understand how treating an underlying infection affects their long-term risk.
