# Cervical Cancer Screening
Cervical cancer screening detects early cell changes caused by HPV before they become cancer. Screening is highly effective and prevents most cervical cancers.

## Why Cervical Screening Matters
- HPV causes nearly all cervical cancers.
- Screening detects precancerous changes early.
- Treatment of early changes prevents cancer.

## Who Should Be Screened?
### Ages 25–29
- Pap test every 3 years.

### Ages 30–65
- HPV test every 5 years
OR
- Pap test every 3 years
OR
- HPV + Pap co-testing every 5 years

### Ages 65+
Stop screening if:
- adequate prior screening
- no history of high-grade lesions

### Higher Risk
More frequent screening if:
- immunocompromised
- history of high-grade dysplasia
- DES exposure

## Screening Tools
### Pap Test
Detects abnormal cervical cells.

### HPV Test
Detects high-risk HPV strains that cause cancer.

### Co-Testing
Pap + HPV together.

## Common Findings
### Normal
Routine screening continues.

### ASC-US
Minor changes; may need HPV testing.

### LSIL / HSIL
Precancerous changes; may need colposcopy.

### HPV Positive
Follow-up depends on age and HPV type.

## Screening Barriers
- transportation barriers
- financial barriers
- provider bias
- cultural safety concerns
- fear or discomfort
- limited access in rural areas
