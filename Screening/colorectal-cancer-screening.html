Colorectal Cancer Screening

Reviewed by Dr. Derrick J. Beech, MD, FACS, Surgical Oncologist
Last updated: September 2026

Colorectal cancer screening detects precancerous polyps and early cancers before symptoms appear. Screening dramatically reduces mortality and is one of the most effective cancer prevention tools available.

Why Colorectal Screening Matters

Most colorectal cancers begin as polyps. Screening removes polyps before they turn into cancer, leads to highly curable disease when caught early, and reduces colorectal cancer deaths by up to 60%.

Who Should Be Screened?

Average Risk

Begin screening at age 45. Continue until age 74. Screening options include stool tests or colonoscopy.

Higher Risk

Start earlier (age 40 or sooner) if: family history of colorectal cancer or advanced polyps, personal history of polyps, inflammatory bowel disease (Crohn's, ulcerative colitis), or hereditary syndromes (Lynch, FAP).

Screening Options

FIT Test (Fecal Immunochemical Test)

Annual stool test. Detects hidden blood. Non-invasive and highly effective.

Colonoscopy

Every 10 years if normal. Detects and removes polyps during the same procedure. Gold standard for screening.

Flexible Sigmoidoscopy

Every 5 years. Examines lower colon only.

Stool DNA Test (e.g., FIT-DNA)

Every 3 years. Detects blood plus DNA changes.

Common Findings

Polyps: adenomas (precancerous), hyperplastic (usually benign), sessile serrated lesions (higher risk). Cancer: if detected early, treatment outcomes are excellent.
