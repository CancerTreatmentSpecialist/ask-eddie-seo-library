Testicular Cancer Screening

Reviewed by Dr. Derrick J. Beech, MD, FACS, Surgical Oncologist
Last updated: September 2026

Testicular cancer is the most common cancer in young men (ages 15–35). Screening focuses on awareness and early detection rather than routine imaging.

Why Testicular Screening Matters

It's highly curable when found early, is often detected by patients themselves, and its rapid growth means early action is important.

Who Should Be Screened?

Average Risk

Monthly self-exam. Report any changes promptly.

Higher Risk

More awareness if: history of undescended testicle, family history of testicular cancer, or prior testicular cancer.

Screening Tools

Self-Exam

Check for lumps, firmness, swelling, heaviness, or pain or discomfort.

Clinical Exam

Performed during routine healthcare visits.

Ultrasound

Used when a lump or abnormality is found.

Common Findings

Benign: epididymal cysts, hydroceles, varicoceles.
Suspicious: firm, painless lump; swelling without injury; sudden heaviness.
