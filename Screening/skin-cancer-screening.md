# Skin Cancer Screening
Skin cancer screening helps detect melanoma and non-melanoma skin cancers early, when treatment is most effective. Screening is especially important for people with fair skin, high sun exposure, or a history of tanning.

## Why Skin Screening Matters
- Skin cancer is the most common cancer.
- Melanoma can be deadly if not caught early.
- Early detection leads to excellent outcomes.

## Who Should Be Screened?
### Average Risk
- Annual skin check with a healthcare provider.
- Monthly self-exams.

### Higher Risk
More frequent screening if:
- fair skin, freckles, red or blonde hair
- history of sunburns or tanning beds
- family history of melanoma
- many moles or atypical moles
- immunosuppression

## Screening Tools
### Full-Body Skin Exam
Performed by a clinician; looks for suspicious lesions.

### Dermoscopy
Magnified tool that helps identify melanoma features.

### Total Body Photography
Used for patients with many moles.

## ABCDEs of Melanoma
- **A**symmetry
- **B**order irregularity
- **C**olor variation
- **D**iameter > 6 mm
- **E**volving (changing)

## Common Findings
### Benign
- moles
- seborrheic keratoses
- skin tags

### Suspicious
- irregular moles
- rapidly changing lesions
- bleeding or crusting spots

## Screening Barriers
- limited access to dermatology
- cost concerns
- provider bias
- lack of awareness
- rural access challenges
