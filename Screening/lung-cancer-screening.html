Lung Cancer Screening

Reviewed by Dr. Derrick J. Beech, MD, FACS, Surgical Oncologist
Last updated: September 2026

Lung cancer screening detects cancer early, when treatment is most effective. Screening is recommended for people at higher risk due to smoking history or age.

Why Lung Screening Matters

Lung cancer is often diagnosed late. Low-dose CT (LDCT) can detect cancer early, and early detection significantly improves survival.

Who Should Be Screened?

High-Risk Individuals

Annual low-dose CT scan if: age 50–80 AND 20+ pack-year smoking history AND currently smoke or quit within the last 15 years.

Not Recommended For

People with no smoking history, people under 50, or people with major health issues limiting treatment.

Screening Tool

Low-Dose CT (LDCT)

A quick, painless scan that uses lower radiation than standard CT and detects small nodules early.

Common Findings

Benign nodules: most nodules are not cancer.
Suspicious nodules: require follow-up imaging or biopsy.

Lung-RADS Categories

  • 1–2: benign
  • 3: probably benign; short-term follow-up
  • 4: suspicious; further testing needed
