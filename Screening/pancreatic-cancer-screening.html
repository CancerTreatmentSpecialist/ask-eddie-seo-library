Pancreatic Cancer Screening

Reviewed by Dr. Derrick J. Beech, MD, FACS, Surgical Oncologist
Last updated: September 2026

Pancreatic cancer screening is not recommended for the general population. It is reserved for people with strong genetic or family risk.

Why Pancreatic Screening Matters

Pancreatic cancer is often diagnosed late. High-risk individuals benefit from early detection, though screening requires specialized imaging.

Who Should Be Screened?

High-Risk Individuals Only

Screening considered if: BRCA1/2, PALB2, CDKN2A, STK11, or Lynch syndrome; two or more first-degree relatives with pancreatic cancer; or known hereditary pancreatic cancer syndromes.

Not Recommended For

Average-risk individuals, or people without genetic or family risk.

Screening Tools

  • MRI/MRCP — non-invasive imaging of pancreas and ducts
  • Endoscopic ultrasound (EUS) — highly sensitive; used in specialized clinics
  • Genetic counseling — essential for determining eligibility

Common Findings

Benign: cysts, inflammation, fatty changes.
Suspicious: solid masses, concerning cyst features.

How AskEddy Helps
