# Colorectal Cancer Screening
Colorectal cancer screening detects precancerous polyps and early cancers before symptoms appear. Screening dramatically reduces mortality and is one of the most effective cancer prevention tools available.

## Why Colorectal Screening Matters
- Most colorectal cancers begin as polyps.
- Screening removes polyps before they turn into cancer.
- Early detection leads to highly curable disease.
- Screening reduces colorectal cancer deaths by up to 60%.

## Who Should Be Screened?
### Average Risk
- Begin screening at age 45.
- Continue until age 74.
- Screening options include stool tests or colonoscopy.

### Higher Risk
Start earlier (age 40 or sooner) if:
- family history of colorectal cancer or advanced polyps
- personal history of polyps
- inflammatory bowel disease (Crohn’s, ulcerative colitis)
- hereditary syndromes (Lynch, FAP)

## Screening Options
### FIT Test (Fecal Immunochemical Test)
- Annual stool test.
- Detects hidden blood.
- Non-invasive and highly effective.

### Colonoscopy
- Every 10 years if normal.
- Detects and removes polyps during the same procedure.
- Gold standard for screening.

### Flexible Sigmoidoscopy
- Every 5 years.
- Examines lower colon only.

### Stool DNA Test (e.g., FIT-DNA)
- Every 3 years.
- Detects blood + DNA changes.

## Common Findings
### Polyps
- Adenomas (precancerous)
- Hyperplastic (usually benign)
- Sessile serrated lesions (higher risk)

### Cancer
If detected early, treatment outcomes are excellent.

## Screening Barriers
- transportation barriers
- financial barriers
- provider bias
- fear of colonoscopy
- cultural safety concerns
- limited access in rural areas
