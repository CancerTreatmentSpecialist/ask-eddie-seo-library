Cervical Cancer Screening

Reviewed by Dr. Derrick J. Beech, MD, FACS, Surgical Oncologist
Last updated: September 2026

Cervical cancer screening detects early cell changes caused by HPV before they become cancer. Screening is highly effective and prevents most cervical cancers.

Why Cervical Screening Matters

HPV causes nearly all cervical cancers. Screening detects precancerous changes early, and treatment of early changes prevents cancer.

Who Should Be Screened?

Ages 25–29

Pap test every 3 years.

Ages 30–65

HPV test every 5 years, or Pap test every 3 years, or HPV + Pap co-testing every 5 years.

Ages 65+

Stop screening if there has been adequate prior screening and no history of high-grade lesions.

Higher Risk

More frequent screening if immunocompromised, history of high-grade dysplasia, or DES exposure.

Screening Tools

  • Pap test — detects abnormal cervical cells
  • HPV test — detects high-risk HPV strains that cause cancer
  • Co-testing — Pap + HPV together

Common Findings

  • Normal — routine screening continues
  • ASC-US — minor changes; may need HPV testing
  • LSIL / HSIL — precancerous changes; may need colposcopy
  • HPV positive — follow-up depends on age and HPV type
