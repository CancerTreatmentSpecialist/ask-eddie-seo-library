Skin Cancer Screening

Reviewed by Dr. Derrick J. Beech, MD, FACS, Surgical Oncologist
Last updated: September 2026

Skin cancer screening helps detect melanoma and non-melanoma skin cancers early, when treatment is most effective. Screening is especially important for people with fair skin, high sun exposure, or a history of tanning.

Why Skin Screening Matters

Skin cancer is the most common cancer. Melanoma can be deadly if not caught early, but early detection leads to excellent outcomes.

Who Should Be Screened?

Average Risk

Annual skin check with a healthcare provider, plus monthly self-exams.

Higher Risk

More frequent screening if: fair skin, freckles, red or blonde hair; history of sunburns or tanning beds; family history of melanoma; many moles or atypical moles; or immunosuppression.

Screening Tools

  • Full-body skin exam — performed by a clinician; looks for suspicious lesions
  • Dermoscopy — magnified tool that helps identify melanoma features
  • Total body photography — used for patients with many moles

ABCDEs of Melanoma

  • Asymmetry
  • Border irregularity
  • Color variation
  • Diameter > 6 mm
  • Evolving (changing)

Common Findings

Benign: moles, seborrheic keratoses, skin tags.
Suspicious: irregular moles, rapidly changing lesions, bleeding or crusting spots.
