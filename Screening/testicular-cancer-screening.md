# Testicular Cancer Screening
Testicular cancer is the most common cancer in young men (ages 15–35). Screening focuses on awareness and early detection rather than routine imaging.

## Why Testicular Screening Matters
- Highly curable when found early.
- Often detected by patients themselves.
- Rapid growth means early action is important.

## Who Should Be Screened?
### Average Risk
- Monthly self-exam.
- Report any changes promptly.

### Higher Risk
More awareness if:
- history of undescended testicle
- family history of testicular cancer
- prior testicular cancer

## Screening Tools
### Self-Exam
Check for:
- lumps
- firmness
- swelling
- heaviness
- pain or discomfort

### Clinical Exam
Performed during routine healthcare visits.

### Ultrasound
Used when a lump or abnormality is found.

## Common Findings
### Benign
- epididymal cysts
- hydroceles
- varicoceles

### Suspicious
- firm, painless lump
- swelling without injury
- sudden heaviness

## Screening Barriers
- stigma or embarrassment
- lack of awareness
- provider bias
- limited access in rural areas
