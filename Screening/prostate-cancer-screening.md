# Prostate Cancer Screening
Prostate cancer screening helps detect cancer early, when treatment is most effective and survival rates are highest. Screening is especially important for Black men and anyone with a family history of prostate cancer.

## Why Prostate Screening Matters
Early detection reduces:
- advanced-stage diagnosis
- need for aggressive treatment
- mortality
- complications from late detection

## Who Should Be Screened?
### Average Risk
- Begin discussion at age 50
- PSA blood test every 1–2 years

### Higher Risk
Start earlier (age 40–45) if:
- Black ancestry
- father, brother, or son with prostate cancer
- known genetic risk (BRCA1/2, HOXB13)

## Screening Tools
### PSA Test (Prostate-Specific Antigen)
Primary screening tool.
Higher PSA can indicate:
- cancer
- infection
- inflammation
- benign enlargement

### Digital Rectal Exam (DRE)
Helps detect lumps or irregularities.

### MRI (for elevated PSA)
Used to guide biopsy decisions.

### Prostate Biopsy
Confirms diagnosis when PSA or MRI is concerning.

## PSA Levels Explained
- PSA < 2.5: generally low risk
- PSA 2.5–4: borderline; may need repeat testing
- PSA > 4: higher risk; MRI or biopsy may be recommended
- Rapid rise over time is more concerning than a single number

## Common Patient Questions
### “Does a high PSA mean cancer?”
Not always — infection, inflammation, or enlargement can raise PSA.

### “Can I avoid biopsy?”
MRI can help determine whether biopsy is needed.

### “Should Black men screen earlier?”
Yes — Black men have higher incidence and double the mortality.

## Screening Barriers
- transportation barriers
- financial barriers
- provider bias
- fear of biopsy
- cultural safety concerns
- limited access to MRI in rural areas
