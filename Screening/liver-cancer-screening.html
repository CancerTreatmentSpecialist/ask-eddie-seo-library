Liver Cancer Screening

Reviewed by Dr. Derrick J. Beech, MD, FACS, Surgical Oncologist
Last updated: September 2026

Liver cancer screening is recommended for people with cirrhosis or chronic liver disease. Screening detects cancer early, when treatment is more effective.

Why Liver Screening Matters

Cirrhosis significantly increases liver cancer risk. Early detection improves survival, and screening is simple and non-invasive.

Who Should Be Screened?

High-Risk Individuals

Screen every 6 months if: cirrhosis (any cause), chronic hepatitis B (even without cirrhosis), or chronic hepatitis C with advanced fibrosis.

Screening Tools

  • Ultrasound (every 6 months) — primary screening tool for liver cancer
  • AFP blood test — alpha-fetoprotein; used alongside ultrasound
  • MRI or CT — used when ultrasound is unclear or suspicious

Common Findings

Benign: cysts, hemangiomas, fatty liver changes.
Suspicious: solid liver masses, rising AFP levels.
